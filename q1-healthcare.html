Episode 01
Christopher Longhurst, MD, MS
CEO, Seattle Children's Hospital
Clinical Leadership
Mission control centers, AI-powered ambient scribes, and restoring humanism in medicine. Dr. Longhurst leads one of the country's premier children's hospitals and has made deploying AI to give doctors time back a personal mission.

Episode 02
Julia Adler-Milstein, PhD
Professor & Chief, Clinical Informatics, UCSF
Health Policy
AI tools are only as good as the humans overseeing them — and Dr. Adler-Milstein's research shows we may be asking clinicians to do something humans are genuinely bad at. She studies how electronic health systems evolve and who really benefits.

Episode 03
Gabriel Wardi, MD
Critical Care Physician & AI Researcher, UC San Diego
Emergency & Critical Care
His deep learning sepsis prediction model reduced mortality by 17%. Dr. Wardi works at the intersection of emergency medicine and AI research, building tools that make life-or-death decisions in real time.
♫ Spotify ♫ Apple ▶ YouTube
Episode 04
Natalie Pageler, MD
Division Chief, Clinical Informatics, Stanford Medicine
Pediatric Informatics
Decisions made in childhood can echo for a lifetime. Dr. Pageler designs AI tools for pediatric care at Stanford, where the stakes — in both directions — are enormous. She explores what it takes to build tools clinicians actually trust.
♫ Spotify ♫ Apple ▶ YouTube
Episode 05
Erin Holve, PhD, MPH, MPP
Executive Director, PCORI
Outcomes Research
Dr. Holve helped write HIPAA, the federal law protecting your medical privacy. Now she's watching AI test every boundary of it. She directs a $4B+ research institute focused on what patients actually need — not just what's technically possible.
♫ Spotify ♫ Apple ▶ YouTube
Episode 06
Mayank Goel, PhD
Associate Professor, CMU School of Computer Science
Passive Sensing & HCI
What if your AirPods could tell you were getting sick three days before you felt anything? Dr. Goel builds the technology that makes passive health monitoring possible using everyday devices — and he's never treated a patient.
