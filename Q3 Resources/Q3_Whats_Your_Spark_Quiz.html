What's Your Spark?

Find the data and behavior path that fits how your mind works  ·  For students navigating what's next
Student-Created Podcast pathsofcuriosity.com

Data isn't one thing, and neither are the careers around it. Some people want to understand how people really behave. Others want to test ideas and measure what works. Some want to make sure data systems are fair and accountable. Others want to translate what the data means so the rest of us can understand it. All of these matter. This quiz helps you figure out where you might fit.

What Your Spark Means
🧠
Spark A — People & Behavior
You want to understand why people do what they do

You're fascinated by the human side of data. Before you trust a number, you want to know what real people were thinking, doing, and feeling when it was created.

Behavioral Science Technology Ethnography Human-Centered Design
Start here: Watch how people use something at school and ask why. Take psychology or economics. Interview someone about a decision they made. Listen to Episodes 1 and 3.
📊
Spark B — Data & Experiments
You want to test it and measure it

You see questions that can be tested. You want to design the experiment, build the model, and ask: what does the evidence actually say? You're drawn to problems that need rigor.

Experimental Design Data Science Decision Analytics
Start here: Learn statistics and basic Python. Run a simple A/B test with friends. Analyze a public dataset (Kaggle, government data). Listen to Episodes 1 and 6.
⚖️
Spark C — Power & Policy
You want to make sure data systems are fair

You ask who benefits before anyone asks how it works. You think about fairness, accountability, and who holds power when more of life gets measured, scored, and automated.

Fair Lending & Fintech Policy Critical Data Studies Technology & Society
Start here: Study fair lending and algorithmic bias cases. Pick a "smart" product and trace who benefits from its data. Listen to Episodes 2, 4, and 5.
🎙️
Spark D — Communicating Ideas
You want to translate what the data means

You know that research only matters if people can understand it. You're a translator, between researchers and the public, between what the numbers show and what people think they show.

Data & Society Communication Science Journalism Education & Media
Start here: Write an explainer on a surprising research finding. Create a podcast, video, or visual series. Practice translating one data idea per week. Listen to Episodes 3 and 5.
