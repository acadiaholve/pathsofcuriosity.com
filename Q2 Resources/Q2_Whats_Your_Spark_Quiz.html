What's Your Spark?

Find the law and civic life path that fits how your mind works  ·  For students navigating what's next
Student-Created Podcast pathsofcuriosity.com

AI isn't one thing, and neither are the careers around it. Some people want to defend people's rights. Others want to build and test the tools. Some want to write the rules that keep technology accountable. Others want to investigate and explain what's happening so the rest of us can understand it. All of these matter. This quiz helps you figure out where you might fit.

What Your Spark Means
⚖️
Spark A — Advocacy & Justice
You want to defend people's rights

You're drawn to the human side of law and technology. You want to stand up for people when a system treats them unfairly, whether that system is a court, a company, or an algorithm.

Civil Liberties Law Civil Rights & Algorithmic Justice Worker Advocacy
Start here: Join mock trial or debate. Read a summary of a landmark privacy case. Volunteer with a community or civil rights group. Listen to Episodes 1 and 5.
💻
Spark B — Legal Tech & Data
You want to build and test the tools

You see patterns where others see piles of paper. You want to build, test, and question the tools that sort evidence and speed up legal work, and to ask: does this actually work, and how would we know?

eDiscovery Legal Technology Legal Operations
Start here: Learn Python and basic text analysis. Try searching a public records dataset. Study logic and statistics. Listen to Episodes 3 and 6.
🏛️
Spark C — Policy & Governance
You want to design the rules

You ask what the rules should be before anyone asks what the technology can do. You think about who has power, who is left out, and how institutions can keep up with fast-moving technology.

Technology Policy Labor Policy Internet Governance
Start here: Follow a local technology policy debate. Write a one-page policy memo. Attend a city council meeting. Listen to Episodes 2 and 4.
🎙️
Spark D — Research & Storytelling
You want to investigate and explain

You know that a ruling or a rule only matters if people understand it. You're a translator, between courts and communities, between researchers and the public, between what the law says and what people think it says.

Legal Research Civic Tech Communication Education & Media
Start here: Write an explainer on a recent court ruling. Interview someone about a system that affects them. Start a podcast, blog, or video series. Listen to Episodes 3 and 4.
